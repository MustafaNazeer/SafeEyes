# Alert validation: measured alert level behavior and the yawn signal

Two measurements that close open items from the [safety review](safety-review.md):
the false alarm rate of the full alert stack on labeled sequences, and a held out
validation of the geometric yawn signal on YawDD. Every number here traces to
`alert-validation-sweep.json`, `alert-validation-metrics.json`, or
`yawdd-yawn-metrics.json` in this directory and is regenerated by
`safeeyes-validate-alerts` and the scoring utilities in
`safeeyes.temporal.yawn_validation`. SafeEyes is an assistive prototype, not a
medical device, and makes no diagnostic or safety of life guarantees; nothing
here claims otherwise.

## Method: alert level replay

The deployed temporal checkpoint is replayed over per frame feature sequences
extracted from the UTA-RLDD clips at the live cadence (about 10 features per
second). Each sequence streams through the same rolling window, classifier, and
tiered state machine the live loop runs, so the measurement exercises the real
decision path, not an approximation of it. Ground truth is the whole clip label;
a false alarm is an excursion of the alert tier to at or above a threshold tier
during a clip labeled alert or low vigilance. The headline threshold is AUDIBLE
or above, since the audible cue is the first intervention a driver cannot
ignore; the stricter and looser definitions are reported beside it.

The state machine parameters (escalation steps, de escalation steps, alarm
persistence) were tuned on the 38 train subjects only, under a selection rule
fixed before the sweep ran: among parameter sets whose train drowsy clip
detection rate is at least that of the shipped defaults, the lowest false alarms
per hour wins, with ties broken by faster median time to first alert. The chosen
parameters were then frozen and the 10 held out test subjects were evaluated
exactly once.

Two honest caveats. Replay steps at the extraction cadence of about 10 per
second, while the live loop runs at roughly 11 to 15 frames per second, so a
step count translates to about 0.7 to 1.0 times its replay wall clock on device.
And at the AUDIBLE threshold the alarm persistence parameter cannot affect any
reported number (it only splits ALARM from AUDIBLE), so its swept value of 15 is
a tie resolution artifact, not an evidence based choice; the shipped default of
45 steps remains the reference for ALARM timing.

## Alert level results

Train sweep (112 clips, 64 parameter sets): the shipped defaults (5, 15, 45)
produce 51.75 false alarms per hour on not drowsy clips at a 97.4% drowsy clip
detection rate with a median 19.3 s to first alert. The selected set (8, 40, 15)
halves that to 26.27 per hour at the same 97.4% detection rate, with the median
first alert at 21.8 s.

Frozen single test run (29 clips: 20 not drowsy, 9 drowsy), parameters (8, 40, 15):

| Threshold | False alarms per hour | Not drowsy clips with an alarm | Alert labeled clips only | Drowsy clips detected | Median time to first alert |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| VISUAL or above | 28.92 | 90.0% | 23.89 | 100.0% | 15.6 s |
| AUDIBLE or above | 15.92 | 65.0% | 8.63 | 100.0% | 24.2 s |
| ALARM | 15.92 | 65.0% | 8.63 | 100.0% | 25.6 s |

The headline reading is blunt: the system detected all 9 of 9 drowsy clips
(100.0% on this small test set) with a median 24.2 s to the first audible alert,
but it also raised 15.92 false audible alarms per hour across the not drowsy
test footage, and 65.0% of not drowsy clips saw at least one. On clips labeled
alert (excluding low vigilance) the rate is 8.63 per hour, still far above what
any driver would tolerate. The ALARM row matching the AUDIBLE row reflects the
tie resolution artifact noted above: with an alarm persistence of 15 steps,
essentially every committed audible excursion escalates.

These numbers measure the deployed classifier plus the alert stage as a system.
They confirm the safety review's expectation in one direction (the alert stage
does absorb isolated false windows: 15.92 per hour is well below the raw window
false alarm rate would imply) and refute any hope in the other: debounce tuning
alone cannot rescue a per window false alarm rate of 0.100 into a usable alert
level rate. Following this measurement the runtime defaults adopt the tuned
escalation and de escalation values (8 and 40) while keeping the alarm
persistence of 45; the swept value of 15 is excluded as the tie resolution
artifact described above, so the AUDIBLE tier behavior of the deployed loop
matches the measured rows exactly and the ALARM tier keeps its conservative
timing.

## Method: YawDD yawn signal validation

The yawn signal is a mouth aspect ratio onset counter: a video registers a yawn
when the MAR trace rises to at least the detection threshold. The threshold,
0.617 (exactly 0.616703), is the 99th percentile of all per frame MAR values
across the UTA train subjects, fixed and committed before any YawDD data was
listed, extracted, or scored, so YawDD functions as a purely held out test set.
YawDD videos were processed with the same perception path as everything else,
at the same cadence. YawDD yawns are deliberate, performed on request; that
caveat attaches to every number below. The dataset is used under its research
license and cited below.

Mirror camera set, 320 videos, 115 of them yawning (including the 13 combined
talking and yawning videos): video level precision 46.7%, recall 99.1%. Per
category, the signal fired on 101 of 102 pure yawning videos and all 13 combined
videos, but also on 80 of 100 pure talking videos (a talking false positive rate
of 80.0%) and 50 of 105 normal driving videos. Talking was the designed hard
negative and it defeated the geometric signal decisively.

Dash camera set, 29 videos: per the dataset's own documentation every dash video
contains a yawning scene alongside normal driving and talking, so only recall is
defined at video level; the signal registered a yawn in 29 of 29 (100.0%). Given
the mirror result, this mostly restates that the signal fires readily.

A post hoc precision and recall curve over thresholds from 0.2 to 1.0 is
recorded in `yawdd-yawn-metrics.json` for context. It is descriptive only; the
reported operating point is the preregistered threshold.

## Decisions

- The measured alert level false alarm rate (15.92 per hour at the audible tier,
  beside the 100.0% drowsy clip detection rate on this test set) keeps the
  reliability claim blocked; see the [safety review](safety-review.md) for the
  standing verdict.
- The geometric yawn signal, as the bare threshold crossing rule measured here,
  fails its acceptance bar (0.70 video level precision and recall; measured
  46.7% precision at 99.1% recall over this 320 video population). In that form
  it cannot distinguish yawning from talking at video level. It remains one
  input feature among five to the temporal classifier, where it is not asked to
  stand alone. Requiring the opening to last a minimum duration was tried next,
  together with a trained classifier over mouth crops, on a subject independent
  split of the Mirror set; the measured outcome and the decision not to deploy
  the trained model are in [yawn-model-card.md](yawn-model-card.md) and
  [ADR 0006](../adr/0006-geometric-duration-rule-over-mouth-crop-cnn.md).
